## 1. Purpose

This document defines a governed, reusable AMF naming capability. It prepares—not executes—the future selection of the first external consumer-facing identity for the approved global short-form territory: historical POV narration plus AI-generated emotional/fantasy storytelling, beginning with Instagram Reels and potentially expanding to YouTube Shorts and TikTok.

It does not generate, rank, search, verify, approve, or reserve any name. `STRATEGY_COUNCIL_V2_PHASE` remains `CLOSED`; no canonical Council artifact is changed.

## 2. Naming Scope and Architecture Boundaries

Each naming brief must declare exactly which identity layer is in scope. A string proposed for one layer is not implicitly a decision for another.

| Layer | Role | V1 treatment |
|---|---|---|
| AI Media Factory | Internal company, platform, and operating-system identity | Presumed internal unless the owner explicitly changes that decision. It is not automatically the public brand. |
| Consumer-facing master brand | Public identity audiences recognize across a portfolio | Primary possible V1 target; owner must first confirm this architecture. |
| Channel account name | Platform-facing account identity on a specific destination | May equal the master brand or be a governed platform adaptation; it is not automatically a new brand. |
| Content series / pillar name | Label for a recurring editorial format or pillar | Out of V1 unless separately requested; it must not silently become the master brand or account name. |

The future naming brief must include `namingObjectType`, `parentIdentity`, `allowedRelationshipToParent`, and `platformScope`. Contracts and UI/workflow labels must use these distinct terms, preventing company/brand/channel/series conflation.

## 3. Brand Architecture Decision Framework

No architecture is selected by this preparation. The owner must choose or authorize a provisional hypothesis before candidate generation.

| Option | Structure | Strategic implication | Key risk for the approved two-pillar territory |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Single master consumer brand | One public identity across current and future platforms; both pillars sit beneath it | Efficient recall, shared audience learning, simplest initial account system | The identity must remain broad enough for both pillars. |
| B. Master brand with pillar sub-brands | Parent consumer identity plus separately branded pillar expressions | Supports distinct editorial worlds while retaining portfolio linkage | Early complexity can dilute a small pilot. |
| C. Independent channel brands by pillar | Separate public identities and accounts for each pillar | Strongest pillar-specific positioning | Splits budget, learning, audience, and governance; may prematurely separate related storytelling. |
| D. Hybrid | Shared master identity with selectively differentiated channels or series | Preserves optionality | Needs explicit rules to avoid accidental drift into B or C. |

Evaluation must test whether an architecture can truthfully represent both historical POV and fantasy/emotional storytelling. A history-only identity is a deliberate owner choice, never a default inference.

## 4. Naming Principles and Weight Selection

Future briefs select a prioritized subset and documented weights; these principles are not equal by default:

- Global comprehension, pronunciation, and spelling simplicity.
- Memorability, distinctiveness, emotional resonance, and visual brandability.
- Compatibility with short-form social use and practical account naming.
- Scalability across both approved pillars, platforms, markets/languages, and future products.
- Accuracy: not misleading, falsely official, narrowly historical by accident, or platform-dependent.
- Cultural/linguistic care and avoidance of obvious negative associations.
- Sufficient specificity without generic keyword stuffing or explanation-dependent meaning.

The owner approves the weighting profile (for example, whether distinctiveness outranks immediate descriptiveness) and any non-negotiable principles before generation.

## 5. Naming Territories

Territories are generation directions, not candidate names.

| Territory | Strengths | Weaknesses | AMF / over-specialization fit | Scalability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evocative | Emotion and recall without literal description | May be vague or conflict-prone | Strong if it can hold both pillars | High |
| Invented | Potentially distinctive and protectable | Pronunciation/spelling burden | Good for a broad master identity; needs language review | High |
| Symbolic | Supports visual systems and layered meaning | May require explanation | Good if symbolism is not tied to one era | High |
| Narrative | Signals storytelling clearly | Can become generic | Strong for both pillars when not genre-locked | Medium-high |
| Cinematic | Fits visual-first short-form presentation | May overpromise production form | Good cross-pillar fit | Medium-high |
| Temporal | Can connect past, time, and transformation | Easily becomes history-only | Conditional; screen for fantasy breadth | Medium |
| Mythic | Emotional/fantasy affinity and archetypal depth | Can underrepresent historical POV or imply a religion/culture | Conditional; heightened cultural review | Medium-high |
| Archival | Signals historical texture and credibility | Risks sounding institutional or history-only | Low-to-conditional for the two-pillar master identity | Medium |
| Emotional | Directly supports feeling-led stories | May lack distinctiveness | Strong if balanced with narrative scope | High |
| Hybrid coined-word | Can join compatible conceptual signals | Can become opaque or hard to spell | Good only after linguistic screening | High |
| Abstract/master-brand | Broad and extension-ready | Requires stronger brand-building to gain meaning | Strong default exploration class, not a decision | High |

## 6. Elimination Rules

A candidate is removed before weighted scoring when it is excessively tied to one battle, era, country, language, platform, or pillar without owner authorization; difficult to pronounce or spell internationally; generic keyword stuffing; an obvious imitation; falsely official or historical-authority implying; misleading; explanation-dependent; excessively long; visually unsuitable; or has an identified severe cultural, linguistic, or negative-association risk. Unverified conflict, domain, or handle status is an unknown—not a claim of availability.

## 7. Lean Future Role Set

V1 should use four roles, with verification activated only after owner shortlist selection:

1. **Naming Strategist:** converts the approved brief into territories and candidates.
2. **Brand Strategist:** assesses architecture, portfolio, and future-expansion fit.
3. **Linguistic/Cultural and Growth Reviewer:** combined lean review for pronunciation, apparent meaning, cultural concerns, memorability, and account/search usability; may recommend escalation to distinct specialists for material risk.
4. **Naming Synthesizer:** records screened comparisons and an owner-facing shortlist; cannot approve a winner.
5. **Verification Agent (conditional):** gathers bounded preliminary external evidence only after authorization.

When execution is authorized, roles 1–4 requiring business judgment use real LLM execution with recorded source/provenance. Deterministic code may normalize, deduplicate, validate schemas, calculate prescribed scores, persist artifacts, and enforce gates; it must not manufacture strategic naming judgment. The owner remains the only final decision-maker.

## 8. Bounded Future Execution Workflow

`Naming Brief → Candidate Generation → Deterministic Deduplication → Strategic Screening → Linguistic/Growth Screening → Brandability and Portfolio Review → Owner Shortlist Review → Authorized Preliminary Verification → Final Owner Review → Owner Decision`

The workflow is fail-closed on missing owner-approved brief, missing architecture decision, absent lineage, or unrecorded unknowns. It never treats a score, a generated rationale, or preliminary research as final authority.

Recommended low-cost bound: explore 5–7 territories, 4–6 candidates per territory, and a maximum of 30 unique candidates in an initial round. Screen to 8–12 for owner review, then verify no more than 3–5 owner-selected finalists. Allow at most two regeneration rounds after the initial round; a full rejection becomes an explicit owner instruction with revised brief/weights, not unlimited retrying.

## 9. Scoring Model

Use a 1–5 anchored score per dimension, with `0` or `N/A` reserved for unavailable evidence rather than invented confidence. Suggested dimensions are strategic fit, memorability, pronunciation, spelling simplicity, distinctiveness, emotional resonance, visual brandability, cross-platform suitability, pillar scalability, future expansion, cultural/linguistic safety, and search/discoverability usability.

The approved NamingBrief carries weights totaling 100 and records the rationale. Scores produce a transparent comparison aid only. Hard-fail criteria in section 6, unresolved material safety concerns, and required verification findings override arithmetic. Qualitative reviewer notes must record ambiguity, tradeoffs, assumptions, and dissent. The highest numeric score is never an automatic winner.

## 10. Verification Policy

Verification begins only for an owner-approved shortlist and under an explicit bounded authorization. Preliminary screening may collect exact web-search observations, prominent brand-conflict observations, domain and platform-handle observations, and linguistic/pronunciation ambiguity. Evidence must state source, query/check type, timestamp, region/jurisdiction limitations, result, and unknowns.

Preliminary research is not legal trademark clearance. AMF must never claim trademark availability, legal clearance, domain availability, or handle availability without contemporaneous evidence; it must never present a web search as a legal opinion. Formal trademark clearance, jurisdiction selection, and legal advice require an appropriately authorized professional process outside this capability.

## 11. Domain and Handle Policy

Exact readable identity alignment is preferred, but exact `.com` absence alone does not reject an otherwise strategically superior identity. Evaluate in order: exact primary domain where appropriate; concise, semantically clean alternative domains; consistent platform handles; then transparent, minimal modifiers or separators only where needed. Avoid confusing, misleading, excessively long, or platform-specific adaptations.

Record the canonical public spelling separately from each domain and account variant. A chosen brand is not considered secured until the owner accepts the tradeoff and the relevant verification evidence is current.
