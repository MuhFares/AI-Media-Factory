# Brand Guidelines

This document defines how AI Media Factory (AMF) presents itself and how every asset AMF produces - for its own brands and, later, for customers - is expected to read, look, and hold up. It is the practical enforcement of AMF's [values.md](./values.md), and the standards here are applied through the [decision-framework.md](./decision-framework.md) when a call is not obvious.

These are not aspirations. The Writer, SEO, and Publisher agents enforce them on every asset. See [How the agents apply these guidelines](#how-the-agents-apply-these-guidelines) at the end.

## Brand voice and tone

AMF's voice is **confident, clear, grounded, and expert-but-approachable**. We show rather than tell. We do not hype. The cadence is relaxed and quick - short where short works, never padded.

The five attributes, made concrete:

**Confident.** We state what is true plainly. We do not hedge with "we think maybe" or inflate with "revolutionary." Confidence is calm, not loud.

**Clear.** One idea per sentence where possible. Plain words over jargon. If a specialist term earns its place, we define it once and move on.

**Grounded.** Claims are backed by something - a number, an example, a demonstrated result. We would rather show a single concrete outcome than assert a big one.

**Expert-but-approachable.** We know the domain deeply and we do not perform it. We explain to a smart peer, not down to a novice and not up to an insider.

**Show, don't tell.** Instead of "our system is powerful," we show what it produced. Instead of "easy to use," we show the three steps. Evidence over adjectives.

The cadence is relaxed and quick: we get to the point, we vary sentence length, and we do not fill space. Read anything we publish aloud - if it sounds like a person who knows their subject and respects your time, it is on voice.

## Writing dos and don'ts

### Do

- **Lead with the point.** Say the useful thing first; explain after.
- **Use concrete nouns and active verbs.** "The Writer agent drafts the script" beats "scripts are able to be generated."
- **Show evidence.** Numbers, examples, before/after. One real result outweighs three claims.
- **Define a term once, then use it.** Respect the reader's time and intelligence.
- **Vary sentence length.** A short sentence lands. Longer ones carry the detail. The mix creates the relaxed, quick cadence.
- **Cut ruthlessly.** If a sentence can go without losing meaning, it goes.

### Don't

- **Don't hype.** No "revolutionary," "game-changing," "unleash," "supercharge," "world-class."
- **Don't stack superlatives.** "The best, fastest, most powerful" reads as insecurity, not confidence.
- **Don't hedge into mush.** "We believe this could potentially help" says nothing. Say what is true.
- **Don't use empty intensifiers.** "Very," "really," "extremely," "incredibly" almost always weaken the sentence.
- **Don't bury the point** under throat-clearing preamble.
- **Don't use emojis** in AMF's own voice or brand content.

### Examples

| Off-brand | On-brand |
|---|---|
| "AMF is a revolutionary, game-changing platform that will supercharge your content!" | "AMF runs a media company's worth of production as a set of AI agents. Here is what it made this week." |
| "We believe our tools could potentially help you maybe save some time." | "The pipeline takes a topic and returns a finished video, thumbnail, and description. What took a day takes minutes." |
| "Our incredibly powerful, best-in-class AI writes amazing content." | "The Writer agent drafts the script; the SEO agent shapes it to rank. You review and approve." |
| "Unleash the full potential of your content strategy today!" | "Publish daily without working more hours. Here's the three-step setup." |

The pattern across every row: replace the adjective with the evidence.

## Visual identity direction

AMF's visual identity follows the same principles as its voice: clear, grounded, confident, unhurried. Clean layouts, strong hierarchy, generous space. Nothing loud, nothing cluttered.

The canonical logo, font, and color assets live in [../../assets/](../../assets/). Until those assets are finalized, treat the following as direction:

- **Logos.** Primary and mono marks are stored in [../../assets/logos/](../../assets/logos/README.md). Use the primary mark on light backgrounds and the mono mark where color would compete.
- **Fonts.** A clean, readable typeface pairing - one for headings, one for body - is stored in [../../assets/fonts/](../../assets/fonts/README.md). Legibility takes priority over personality.
- **Colors.** A restrained core palette with a single accent is defined in [../../assets/](../../assets/README.md). Use the accent sparingly, for emphasis rather than decoration.

The rule that governs all visual work: hierarchy and restraint. If an element does not help the reader understand faster, it comes out. The exact tokens are owned by the asset files in [../../assets/](../../assets/); this document owns the intent.

## Naming conventions for owned media brands

AMF runs a portfolio of owned faceless media brands. Their names follow rules that keep the portfolio coherent without making every brand look the same.

- **Each brand name stands on its own.** Owned brands are not sub-brands of AMF and do not carry the AMF name. The audience meets the brand, not the factory behind it.
- **Names are clear about the niche, not clever at its expense.** A viewer should get a rough sense of what the channel is about from the name. Clarity beats wordplay.
- **Names are platform-portable.** A brand runs across YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and a newsletter, so the name must work as a handle everywhere - short enough, available, and free of characters that break on a platform.
- **Names are checked for conflict.** No collision with an existing brand or trademark in the niche. This is a governance check, recorded, not a matter of taste.
- **AMF's own name follows the master voice.** "AI Media Factory" and "AMF" are written consistently; AMF is the operating company, and its house voice is the one defined at the top of this document.

The internal naming and conflict-check process is a decision recorded through the [decision-framework.md](./decision-framework.md), so the reasoning behind each brand name is retrievable later.

## Content quality bar

Every asset AMF publishes - on its own brands or a customer's - must clear this bar before the Publisher agent releases it. Nothing ships that fails any of these gates.

**Brand-safety.** The asset is appropriate, non-defamatory, and free of prohibited or off-brand content. It respects platform policy and the brand's own guidelines. When in doubt, it does not ship - it escalates.

**Citations.** Factual claims are supported. Where the asset asserts something checkable, the source exists and was consulted. We do not publish confident claims we cannot stand behind. This is the practical form of "grounded."

**SEO.** The asset is shaped to be found - title, description, structure, and metadata are sound for its platform. Volume that cannot be discovered is wasted volume.

**Quality.** The asset reads, looks, and sounds like the brand at its best: on-voice per the rules above, clean visually, and genuinely useful to the audience. It clears the bar a discerning human would set, not merely the bar of "good enough to publish."

An asset that fails any gate is reworked or held. The bar is the same whether the asset is the first of the day or the thousandth - this is what makes scale safe, and it is the standard enterprise buyers like Elena the Enterprise Content Lead rely on (see [customer-personas.md](./customer-personas.md)).

## How the agents apply these guidelines

These guidelines are enforced by the organization, not left to good intentions.

**The Writer agent** owns voice and tone. It drafts to the voice rules above - confident, clear, grounded, show-don't-tell - and it applies the dos and don'ts directly, avoiding hype, superlatives, and empty intensifiers. Off-voice drafts are revised before they move downstream.

**The SEO agent** owns the discoverability gate. It shapes titles, descriptions, structure, and metadata so the asset can be found, and it enforces the SEO portion of the quality bar without letting optimization pull the writing off-voice.

**The Publisher agent** owns the final gate. It is the last check before release and enforces the full content quality bar - brand-safety, citations, SEO, and quality. It holds or escalates anything that fails rather than shipping it, and it applies the platform-portability and naming rules when an asset goes out under a brand.

Above them, the Executive Brain records material brand decisions and the playbooks codify what consistently works, so the guidelines here get sharper over time rather than drifting. The reasoning behind any hard brand call is preserved through the [decision-framework.md](./decision-framework.md), and the guidelines themselves are downstream of AMF's [values.md](./values.md).

The short version: the voice, the quality bar, and the naming rules are not documentation the team is asked to remember. They are enforced by the agents that produce and publish every asset.
