# Customer Personas

This document defines the five customer personas AI Media Factory (AMF) is built to serve. It describes who they are, the jobs they are trying to get done, the pains that block them, the gains they are chasing, what they use today, what AMF gives them, the pricing tier that fits, and the products they lean on.

For the market context these personas sit inside, see [target-market.md](./target-market.md). For the pricing tiers referenced throughout, see [pricing.md](./pricing.md). For the product surfaces each persona touches, see [products.md](./products.md).

## A note on phasing: Phase 1 has no external customer

AMF is being built inside-out. In **Phase 1**, AMF operates its own portfolio of owned faceless media brands. There is no external buyer in Phase 1 - the "customer" is AMF itself, and the product is proven against real P&L before it is sold to anyone. The personas below become relevant from **Phase 2 onward**, when the same operating system that runs AMF's brands is offered to outside operators.

This matters for how you read the rest of this document. Every "what AMF gives them" claim below is something AMF first demands of itself while running its own brands. We do not sell a capability the internal portfolio has not already used in anger. The phasing is described in more detail in [target-market.md](./target-market.md#go-to-market-by-phase).

## Persona 1: Chris the Creator

**Profile.** Chris runs one or two faceless YouTube channels solo. He is technically comfortable, edits his own videos, and has built a modest audience through consistency. He is not a company. He is one person with a laptop, a content calendar, and more ideas than hours.

**Jobs to be done.**
- Publish consistently on a schedule without burning out.
- Turn a single idea into a video, a Short, and a description that ranks.
- Grow a channel to the point where it pays for itself and then some.

**Pains.**
- The production pipeline (research, script, voiceover, thumbnail, upload, SEO) is a full day of work per video.
- Consistency collapses the moment life gets busy; the algorithm punishes gaps.
- He cannot afford to hire an editor, a writer, and a thumbnail designer.

**Gains.**
- Ship daily instead of weekly without working more hours.
- Keep quality high enough that growth compounds rather than stalls.
- Free his own time for strategy, community, and the parts only he can do.

**Current alternatives.** A stack of disconnected tools - a script generator, a text-to-speech app, a thumbnail template, a scheduling tool - all glued together by hand. Or he simply does everything manually and accepts a slow cadence.

**What AMF gives him.** A single autonomous pipeline that takes a topic and returns a finished, publishable asset set. The Research, Writer, SEO, Thumbnail, Video, and Publisher agents do the work that used to be his whole day. He reviews and approves rather than assembles. See [competitive-advantages.md](./competitive-advantages.md) for why an integrated agent org beats a stack of point tools.

**Pricing tier that fits.** **Creator ($49)** - see [pricing.md](./pricing.md). Chris graduates from Starter once he is publishing often enough that the time saved dwarfs the subscription.

**Products used.** Content production pipeline, thumbnail generation, publishing and scheduling. See [products.md](./products.md).

## Persona 2: Ana the Agency Owner

**Profile.** Ana runs a content agency serving a roster of clients - local businesses, personal brands, a couple of larger accounts. She has a small team of contractors and spends most of her week coordinating rather than creating. Her margins are thin because her costs scale linearly with her client count.

**Jobs to be done.**
- Deliver consistent content for many clients without a proportional headcount increase.
- Keep each client's voice, brand, and platform mix distinct.
- Report results back to clients in a way that justifies the retainer.

**Pains.**
- Every new client means more coordination overhead and more ways for quality to slip.
- Contractor quality is uneven; onboarding is slow.
- She is the bottleneck - nothing ships without her review.

**Gains.**
- Take on more clients without hiring proportionally.
- Standardize quality across every account.
- Turn her expertise into a system rather than a set of Slack messages.

**Current alternatives.** A patchwork of freelancers, project management tools, and her own late nights. Some AI writing tools bolted onto a manual workflow. See the freelancer/agency comparison in [competitive-advantages.md](./competitive-advantages.md#competitive-landscape).

**What AMF gives her.** Multi-brand, multi-platform production native to the system. Each client is a brand with its own voice profile and platform mix, run by the same agent org in parallel. Ana moves from doing the work to governing the system - setting standards, approving playbooks, and reviewing exceptions. The Finance agent keeps per-client unit economics visible so she knows which accounts actually make money.

**Pricing tier that fits.** **Agency ($499)** - see [pricing.md](./pricing.md).

**Products used.** Multi-brand management, content production pipeline, analytics and reporting, publishing. See [products.md](./products.md).

## Persona 3: Marcus the SMB Marketer

**Profile.** Marcus is a one-person marketing function at a small or mid-sized business. He owns the blog, the email list, the social channels, and the pipeline they are supposed to feed. He has a limited budget, no content team, and a boss who wants results measured in leads.

**Jobs to be done.**
- Keep a content marketing engine running across blog, email, and social.
- Produce enough volume to actually move search rankings and pipeline.
- Show marketing's contribution to revenue.

**Pains.**
- He cannot produce enough content alone to matter.
- Agencies are expensive and slow; freelancers are inconsistent.
- Content marketing only works at a volume and consistency he cannot personally sustain.

**Gains.**
- A steady stream of on-brand, SEO-sound content without a team.
- Coverage across formats from one source of truth.
- Budget that goes to output, not overhead.

**Current alternatives.** An AI writing tool for drafts, a freelancer for the occasional bigger piece, and a lot of gaps. Or an agency retainer that eats most of his budget.

**What AMF gives him.** A content marketing engine that runs at agency scale on an SMB budget. Built-in unit-economics discipline - the Finance agent plus model routing - keeps the cost per asset low enough that the math works for a small business. The SEO agent ensures volume does not come at the cost of rank.

**Pricing tier that fits.** **Studio ($199)** - see [pricing.md](./pricing.md). Marcus needs more than a solo creator but does not run many separate brands.

**Products used.** Content production pipeline, SEO, blog and email production, analytics. See [products.md](./products.md).

## Persona 4: Priya the Portfolio Operator

**Profile.** Priya treats faceless media as a business, not a hobby. She runs a portfolio of brands across niches, each a small revenue engine, and she thinks in terms of return on capital and time. She is closest to how AMF itself operates its Phase 1 portfolio, which makes her the sharpest external validation of the product.

**Jobs to be done.**
- Run many brands in parallel without a large team.
- Decide where to invest attention and capital across the portfolio.
- Kill underperformers and double down on winners quickly.

**Pains.**
- Managing many brands manually does not scale past a handful.
- She lacks clean, comparable unit economics across brands.
- Wins in one brand do not automatically transfer to the others.

**Gains.**
- Operate a large portfolio with the effort of running a few brands.
- See true per-brand economics to allocate capital well.
- Codify what works so every brand benefits from every win.

**Current alternatives.** A small ops team, spreadsheets, and heroic personal effort - which caps her at the number of brands she can hold in her head.

**What AMF gives her.** The same operating system AMF uses on its own portfolio. Multi-brand is native. The self-learning data flywheel means a winning tactic discovered in one brand is codified into a playbook and applied across the portfolio - see [competitive-advantages.md](./competitive-advantages.md#the-data-flywheel). The Finance and Analytics agents give her the per-brand P&L she needs to allocate capital.

**Pricing tier that fits.** **Agency ($499)**, moving toward **Enterprise (custom)** as the portfolio grows - see [pricing.md](./pricing.md).

**Products used.** Multi-brand management, full agent org, finance and analytics, playbooks. See [products.md](./products.md).

## Persona 5: Elena the Enterprise Content Lead

**Profile.** Elena leads a content team inside a larger brand. Her constraints are the inverse of Chris's: she has budget and people, but she answers to legal, brand, and compliance. Volume is not her problem; consistency, governance, and defensibility are.

**Jobs to be done.**
- Scale content output across many channels and campaigns.
- Guarantee brand consistency and compliance on every asset.
- Give leadership auditable control over what the brand publishes.

**Pains.**
- Scaling output usually means scaling risk - off-brand or non-compliant content slips through.
- Governance and approval workflows are slow and manual.
- She needs an audit trail for why content was published the way it was.

**Gains.**
- Scale volume without scaling brand and compliance risk.
- Enforce guidelines automatically rather than by review alone.
- A recorded decision trail for every material choice.

**Current alternatives.** A large in-house team plus agencies plus a heavy manual review layer - expensive, slow, and still error-prone at scale.

**What AMF gives her.** Governance built into the operating system. Brand guidelines are enforced by the Writer, SEO, and Publisher agents rather than caught after the fact - see [brand-guidelines.md](./brand-guidelines.md). The Executive Brain records material decisions, and playbooks codify approved patterns, giving Elena the memory and audit trail enterprise governance demands. The content quality bar every asset must clear is defined in [brand-guidelines.md](./brand-guidelines.md#content-quality-bar).

**Pricing tier that fits.** **Enterprise (custom)** - see [pricing.md](./pricing.md).

**Products used.** Full agent org, governance and memory, multi-brand management, analytics and reporting. See [products.md](./products.md).

## Persona summary

| Persona | Core need | Tier | Primary products |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chris the Creator | Scale solo output | Creator ($49) | Production pipeline, thumbnails, publishing |
| Ana the Agency Owner | Many clients, no headcount | Agency ($499) | Multi-brand, analytics, publishing |
| Marcus the SMB Marketer | Content engine on a budget | Studio ($199) | Pipeline, SEO, blog/email |
