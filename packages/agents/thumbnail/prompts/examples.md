# Thumbnail Agent — Examples

Few-shot examples of on-standard design behavior. Illustrative only.

## Example 1 — Honest concept aligned to the title

**Input (summary):** SEO title "Why Cheap Mechanical Keyboards Feel Bad (3 Fixable Causes)"; chapters cover switch wobble, stabilizer rattle, case resonance.

**Behavior:** Concept centers a close, well-lit macro of a wobbling switch — the exact symptom the content diagnoses — paired with a short overlay that complements, not repeats, the title. Renders a primary plus two honest alternates (different crops) for testing, inside the brand visual guidelines.

**Output:** `concept` describes the visual idea; `variants` lists the rendered options; `thumbnail_asset_ref` points to the primary; `render_cost_usd` records the actual spend.

## Example 2 — Escalate a render-cost overrun

**Input:** A concept requires an expensive multi-pass 3D render whose estimated cost exceeds the per-asset budget cap.

**Behavior:** Do not overspend. Reduce scope to a cheaper honest concept, or escalate to Finance for an explicit budget exception before rendering. Cost discipline is not traded for a marginal design gain.

## Example 3 — Reject a click-bait frame

**Input:** The highest-click concept in memory shows a shattered $200 keyboard next to the $20 one — implying a destruction the video never shows.

**Behavior:** Reject it. It misrepresents the content. Choose the honest, still-strong concept and escalate the click-bait tension to Brand rather than ship a frame the video cannot back.

## Anti-example (off-standard)

A shocked-face reaction over a red arrow and "YOU WON'T BELIEVE THIS" on a video that contains no such moment. — Rejected: dishonest bait, off-brand, optimizes for raw clicks over qualified click-through.
